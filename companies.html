How this works

Every agenda item on this site whose official wording names a company or organization (a name ending in Inc., LLC, Corp., Company, P.A. and similar) together with a dollar amount is read by fixed rules, not AI. When the money goes from the government to the company (a contract, award, purchase, task order, change order, grant or incentive), the decision is listed on the company's page.

  • Amounts are what a board approved, not what was paid. They're the figure the wording names for that company, usually a contract's ceiling ("not to exceed"). A contract can end up costing less, or more after change orders. Payment records aren't collected yet.
  • Totals count only decisions that passed. Items still on an agenda, or that failed, are listed but not counted.
  • When one item names several companies, each gets the amount written after its name; if the wording doesn't say, no amount is counted for it.
  • A contract's close-out or final payment restates its final total, so it's listed but not added again.
  • Spellings are joined ("Lennar Homes, LLC" and "Lennar Homes LLC" are one company). Different companies with similar names stay separate. A company's own name is all we match on, so a company that changed its name appears twice.
  • Companies that pay the government (leases of public land, franchise fees) and builders asking for a rezoning are not counted as being paid.
  • Many agendas don't name the company in the item's title (Edgewater's often don't); those decisions aren't here yet.

Worth a second look

These tags state plain facts from the official record. None of them means anything improper happened, and none of them is about a person. They mark the decisions people most often want to read closely.

No competitive bidding
The wording says the purchase was sole source, single source, an emergency purchase, or exempt from competitive bidding. These can be legal and necessary; they're also where price and choice get the least outside checking.
Money added to an existing contract
A change order, amendment or added spending authority on a contract approved earlier.
Approved over a no vote
At least one member voted no.
$1 million or more on the consent agenda
Approved in one vote together with routine items, without its own discussion. Only some governments' records mark consent items (Edgewater, Hernando, Pasco).
A member stepped aside for a conflict of interest
A member abstained or was recused, citing a conflict of interest. Florida law requires this when a vote would benefit the member or a relative or business associate (Fla. Stat. 112.3143).
