How the tags work

The tags come from fixed rules that read the official wording of each decision. No AI is involved, and the official wording is always one tap away.

New bans, fines or penalties

The test: you can't do it now, but you could yesterday. A decision is tagged when its wording describes a ban or prohibition, a moratorium, temporary or permanent (for example on building permits), a new crime, a limit on sales or use, or new or changed fines. Rules the board sets only for itself, such as its own ethics or letterhead policies, aren't tagged.

  • New restriction: adopted at this vote.
  • Restriction in the works: the board asked for one to be drafted, or set it up for a later hearing. It isn't law yet.
  • Restriction extended: an existing moratorium was made longer.
  • On the agenda: proposed, with no recorded vote yet.

Money, land or breaks for companies

A decision is tagged when its wording describes a property-tax exemption or tax refund for a business, an incentive agreement or payment to a named company or corporation, fees or fines forgiven for a named company, public land sold or leased to a company, or a purchase of $100,000 or more from a company without competitive bidding (sole source or emergency). Ordinary bids and contracts aren't tagged. Requests from private residents and gifts to nonprofits aren't tagged either.

What the tags don't say

They say what a decision does, not whether it was right. A ban you support is tagged the same way as one you don't, and many breaks for companies come with promised jobs or services. The rules can miss a decision or, rarely, tag the wrong one. Report a mistake.
