Each square is one city or county government, filled where a vote passed. Each name opens that government's items on the subject. A word after a name says where it stands, read only from recorded votes and meeting dates: passed (a vote passed), coming up (on an agenda for a meeting still to come), motion failed or tie vote; no word means it was on an agenda and no vote is recorded online, which for many governments we follow is always the case. How this works

Every agenda item from the 115 governments we follow is read against a fixed list of subjects, each a set of words in the official wording, with exceptions found by reading real items: a county's own server room isn't a data center decision, and buying a golf cart isn't a golf-cart rule. Proclamations and awareness weeks don't count, and neither do residents' own requests. An item can be about more than one subject. The list was drawn up on November 18, 2026 from the phrases that appeared in the most governments' agendas; subjects with fewer than 3 governments in the last 90 days (or on agendas posted for the next 60) aren't shown here but keep their own pages. The rules can miss an item or, rarely, include one that doesn't fit. Report a mistake.

Some subjects are split into kinds, also by fixed rules over the start of the wording: talks, studies and directions to staff are kept apart from laws, and an item counts as a pause or a ban only when it names an ordinance or resolution. Each government is listed once, under the kind of its newest item that is more than a talk. "Passed" means a vote passed on an item of that kind, not that a law took effect: many ordinances need two readings, and a vote to deny, or to put an item off to a later meeting, is not counted as passed. "For the first time" counts only governments whose records with us start at least a year earlier.

Only governments we collect are counted: see what we cover in Florida. A subject missing here may be on agendas we can't read.
