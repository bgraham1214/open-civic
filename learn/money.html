How county money works

The County Commission sets the property tax rate, passes the budget, and approves the bigger contracts and grants. Here is what those decisions mean.

Property taxes

Florida property taxes are set in mills. One mill is $1 for every $1,000 of a property's taxable value, so a home with $200,000 of taxable value pays $200 for each mill. Each year the Commission proposes a rate, mails the proposed tax to owners (the TRIM notice), holds public hearings and then sets the final rate. Source: Florida Department of Revenue

The budget

The budget decides how the county spends its money for the year. During the year the Commission also moves money between uses and adds money that comes in later, such as grants. These show up as budget changes.

Contracts

When the county buys goods or hires a company for work, larger contracts come to the Commission. The county usually makes companies compete through an invitation to bid (ITB) or a request for proposals (RFP). Our pages show the dollar amount and the company named in the official wording.

The words you'll see

Property tax rate (millage)
Florida property tax rates are set in “mills.” One mill is $1 of tax for every $1,000 of a property's taxable value. A home with $200,000 of taxable value pays $200 per mill. Source
Budget change
Moves money within the county budget or adds money that came in after the budget was set.
Contract or purchase
The county agrees to pay a company or group for goods or work. ITB (invitation to bid) and RFP (request for proposals) are the ways the county asks companies to compete for the job.
Grant
Money given for a specific purpose, either to the county (often from the state or federal government) or from the county to a local group.
Community Development District
A special district a developer can set up under Florida law (Chapter 190) to borrow money for a new community's roads, water and amenities. Homeowners there repay it through an extra yearly charge on their property tax bill. Source
