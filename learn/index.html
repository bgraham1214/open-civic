Words you'll see

Agendas are written in clerk shorthand. Here is what the terms on our pages mean, each with the official source where one exists.

  • How building and land decisions work
  • How county money works

Land and building

Small change to an approved development
The project already has a custom zoning plan (a “planned development”). The owner asks to make a minor change to it, in person before the Commission. Source
Big change to an approved development
The project already has a custom zoning plan (a “planned development”). The owner asks for a major change. A hearing officer holds a public hearing first, then the Commission decides. Source
Rezoning with a custom plan
The owner asks to rezone land as a “planned development”: a custom set of rules for that one project, usually with a site plan showing what goes where. Source
Rezoning to a standard zone
The owner asks to move land into one of the county's standard zones, such as single-family homes or general commercial, with the rules that come with that zone. Source
Rezoning
A request to change the rules for what can be built on a piece of land.
Change to the future land-use map
The county's long-range plan has a map saying how each area may be used in the future and how many homes per acre are allowed at most. This changes that map for one area. A rezoning usually comes after. Source
Change to the wording of the long-range plan
This changes the written policies of the county's long-range land plan, which apply across many properties rather than one. Source
Urban Service Area
The boundary where the county plans to provide city-style services like water and sewer. The county aims to put 80% of new growth inside it. Moving the line lets land outside it ask for more homes or businesses. Source
Giving up a public right-of-way or easement
A request for the county to give up a public road, alley or utility strip it no longer needs, usually to the neighboring owners.
Change to the development rules
The Land Development Code is the county's rulebook for building: lot sizes, setbacks, parking, signs and so on. This changes the rulebook itself, for everyone it applies to. Source
7-OH (a concentrated kratom compound)
7-hydroxymitragynine, or 7-OH, occurs in trace amounts in kratom, a Southeast Asian plant. Products with 7-OH added or concentrated, such as tablets, gummies and drink shots, are strong opioids. The FDA has approved none of them and warns they can cause serious harm. Natural kratom leaf is a separate product. Source

Money

Community Development District
A special district a developer can set up under Florida law (Chapter 190) to borrow money for a new community's roads, water and amenities. Homeowners there repay it through an extra yearly charge on their property tax bill. Source
Property tax rate (millage)
Florida property tax rates are set in “mills.” One mill is $1 of tax for every $1,000 of a property's taxable value. A home with $200,000 of taxable value pays $200 per mill. Source
Contract or purchase
The county agrees to pay a company or group for goods or work. ITB (invitation to bid) and RFP (request for proposals) are the ways the county asks companies to compete for the job.
Grant
Money given for a specific purpose, either to the county (often from the state or federal government) or from the county to a local group.
Incentive or tax break for a business
Public money or a tax break offered to a company, usually in exchange for promised jobs or investment. Under Florida law a county can exempt a new or expanding business from part of its property taxes for up to 10 years, once voters have approved that power in a referendum. Cash incentives are paid under an agreement with the company. Source
Budget change
Moves money within the county budget or adds money that came in after the budget was set.

How meetings work

Consent agenda
A group of routine items approved together in one vote, to save time. Any commissioner can pull an item out to discuss it separately.
Ordinance
A local law. Once adopted it applies to everyone in the county area it covers.
